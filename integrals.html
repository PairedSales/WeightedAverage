01 · The core idea

Build the area from rectangles

To find the area under f(x) between a and b, chop the interval into n slices and stand a rectangle on each one. Their combined area is a Riemann sum — crank n up and the error collapses toward zero. Hover any slice to inspect its contribution.

02 · A subtlety

Area below the axis counts as negative

The integral doesn't measure raw area — it measures signed area. Where the curve dips below the axis, the rectangles have negative height, so that region subtracts. Drag the two handles to move the bounds a and b across a full wave of sin x.

03 · The punchline

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

Let F(x) be the area accumulated under f(t) from 0 up to x. Scrub x and watch: the slope of the area function is the height of the curve. Accumulation and slope — integration and differentiation — are inverse operations.

04 · Reading the symbol

Anatomy of an integral

Leibniz designed the notation to tell the story of the construction: an elongated S for “sum”, the function being sampled, and dx — a slice of width shrunk to the infinitesimal.
